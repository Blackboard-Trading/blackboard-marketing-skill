# Social playbook

This playbook adapts the workflow ideas in [Corey Haines's social skill](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/social/SKILL.md) to Blackboard. It is an original, product-specific guide rather than a copy of that skill.

## Content pillars

Use these as a starting mix, then adjust with actual channel performance and the campaign brief:

1. **How the product works:** orders, position lifecycle, price data, risk, staking, redemption, and the House.
2. **Build and product updates:** verified release notes, interface walkthroughs, behind-the-scenes engineering lessons, and clearly labeled testnet previews.
3. **Market structure education:** explain a mechanic using a clearly labeled hypothetical example; do not present it as a recommendation or prediction.
4. **Community and learning:** answer recurring questions, share user-created material with permission, and explain corrections transparently.
5. **Product invitation:** show who the product is for, what the current deployment supports, and one low-friction next step.

Avoid a fixed promotional percentage. Choose the mix from the current objective and observed audience response.

## Brief intake

Use supplied context first. Capture only what's missing:

- Objective and reader action.
- Audience knowledge level and geography.
- Platform, format, and language.
- Product version, network, and campaign date.
- Claim evidence and its source.
- Visual placement and whether a real screenshot is available.

For financial claims, ask for the source or use an approved current source. Do not fill evidence gaps with plausible numbers.

## Post patterns

Choose the shape that fits the evidence:

- **Mechanic explainer:** reader question → plain-language mechanism → small hypothetical example → caveat → action.
- **Product walkthrough:** task → two or three real UI steps → verified result → where to try it.
- **Release note:** what changed → who it affects → when/where it is available → link or next step.
- **Data observation:** dated source and scope → one observation → what it does and does not show.
- **Founder/build note:** a real event or decision supplied by the team → lesson → consequence for users. Never invent a first-person anecdote.
- **Carousel:** one idea per slide, with an opening promise grounded in content, progressive explanation, one evidence/example slide, and a clear ending.

## Platform adaptation

- **X:** concise claim or question with context; threads should have a visible narrative and useful standalone posts. Verify current character/media rules when needed.
- **LinkedIn:** explain the user problem and product decision with enough context for a professional reader; use a document carousel when sequential teaching helps.
- **Instagram:** make the visual carry the idea; pair a carousel or Reel with a caption that supplies context and caveats.
- **TikTok / Reels / Shorts:** show the outcome or visual idea immediately, then explain one mechanism with captions and a clean close. Script for sound-off viewing.
- **Telegram / community channels:** prioritize useful updates, links, and explicit availability status. Do not present a draft as an official announcement.

These are creative patterns, not claims about current ranking algorithms. Verify current platform rules and channel analytics before making distribution recommendations.

## Natural-copy check

Remove:

- generic claims such as “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” or “the future of trading” without evidence;
- repeated hook formulas, fake urgency, “agree?” prompts, and empty calls for engagement;
- one-sentence-per-line broetry, excessive fragments, and emoji as list markers;
- “it's not X, it's Y” contrast reveals, stock AI phrases, and repeated summaries of the same claim;
- invented testimonials, founder emotions, customer outcomes, volume, returns, or adoption metrics.

Read the post aloud. Replace abstractions with an exact product action or sourced fact. Keep one primary idea per post.

## Image and carousel handoff

Provide a simple art brief with purpose, placement, ratio, visual hierarchy, exact overlay copy, source assets, and what must remain factual. Use the included frontend snapshot for real Blackboard visual references. If a number or UI state is shown, record where it came from and when it was checked.
